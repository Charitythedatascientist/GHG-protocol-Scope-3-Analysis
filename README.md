# Scope 3 Inventory – FY2023 Summary

> **Basis:** GHG Protocol Corporate Value Chain (Scope 3) Standard; Category 15 per PCAF (2022).
> **Units:** All figures in tCO₂e unless stated otherwise.
> **Source:** `Scope3_GHGProtocol_Practice_Dataset.xlsx` (practice dataset, 100 records per category).

# Key Messages

## 1. The footprint is almost entirely downstream and product-driven

FY2023 Scope 3 emissions total **68.1 MtCO₂e**, with two categories accounting for **99.8%** of the total:

* **Category 11 – Use of Sold Products:** 62.4 MtCO₂e (**91.6%**)
* **Category 15 – Investments:** 5.6 MtCO₂e (**8.2%**)

Decarbonisation and assurance efforts should therefore be concentrated on these two categories.

## 2. Fuel-burning products are the single largest lever

Direct combustion of fuel in sold products accounts for **54.7 MtCO₂e**, or **87.7% of Category 11**. Industrial products alone contribute **41.9 MtCO₂e**.

Improving product efficiency and shifting the portfolio towards electrified products would have a substantially greater impact on total Scope 3 emissions than any upstream programme.

## 3. Upstream operations are immaterial by comparison

All eight upstream categories combined account for **89.0 ktCO₂e**, or just **0.13% of the total**.

They remain worth managing for completeness and cost, but they are unlikely to materially change the headline Scope 3 figure.

## 4. The inventory is not yet assurance-ready

Five data-quality flags have been raised. The most significant issues are:

* A spend-based emission factor library that is currently unusable
* A unit error in Category 5
* Weak PCAF data quality for **39% of investment holdings**

Because Categories 11 and 15 dominate the inventory, the reliability of the overall footprint depends heavily on the assumptions and source data underpinning those categories.

# What Drives the Footprint?

## Category 11 – Use of Sold Products

Category 11 contributes **62.4 MtCO₂e**.

* **Fuel combustion during product use:** 54.7 MtCO₂e (**87.7%**)
* **Electricity used by products:** 7.7 MtCO₂e (**12.3%**)

By product group:

* **Industrial products:** 41.9 MtCO₂e
* **Petrol products:** 6.7 MtCO₂e
* **Diesel products:** 6.0 MtCO₂e

## Category 15 – Investments

Category 15 contributes **5.6 MtCO₂e** in financed emissions.

The largest concentrations are:

* **Utilities:** 18.5%
* **Oil & Gas:** 13.0%

Together, these sectors account for approximately **1.75 MtCO₂e**, or **31.5% of Category 15**.

## Logistics and End of Life

Although relatively small in absolute terms, several areas offer clear reduction opportunities:

* **Air freight:** 72.4% of upstream transportation emissions, equivalent to **25.9 ktCO₂e** of 35.7 ktCO₂e
* **Landfill:** 51.6% of end-of-life emissions, equivalent to **7.8 ktCO₂e** of 15.2 ktCO₂e

These areas may offer practical, cost-effective reduction opportunities despite their limited contribution to the overall footprint.

# Data Quality and Assurance Flags

## F1 – Spend-Based Emission Factors Unusable

Spend-based emission factors are roughly **two orders of magnitude below EEIO norms**.

In Category 4, distance-based results are approximately **24,000× higher** than spend-based results.

**Action:** The spend-based method should not be used for reporting until the emission factor library has been rebuilt and validated.

## F2 – Category 5 Unit Error

Waste factors are labelled **per tonne** but have magnitudes consistent with **per kg** factors.

This understates Category 5 by approximately **1,000×**:

* Reported: **1.0 tCO₂e**
* Corrected: approximately **984 tCO₂e**

The impact on the overall Scope 3 total is immaterial, but the issue should be corrected before assurance.

## F3 – Category 1 Method Divergence

The average-data result of **1,820 tCO₂e** is approximately **3.8×** the spend-based result of **476 tCO₂e**.

Only **22% of purchase lines** currently have supplier primary data.

This creates material uncertainty in the Category 1 estimate.

## F4 – Weak PCAF Data Quality

Approximately **39% of Category 15 holdings** score **4–5** under PCAF data quality, indicating modelled or estimated data.

Higher-quality underlying data should be prioritised for the largest and most material exposures.

## F5 – Concentration Risk

Categories 11 and 15 account for **99.8% of the inventory**.

Consequently, the reliability of the overall Scope 3 figure depends disproportionately on the inputs and assumptions used for these categories, including:

* Product lifetime
* Product usage profiles
* Grid emission factors
* EVIC and other investment data inputs

# Recommended Next Steps

## 1. Prioritise Category 11 Assurance

Validate product lifetime, usage and fuel-mix assumptions for **industrial, petrol and diesel products**.

In parallel, establish a product-efficiency and electrification roadmap focused on the largest emissions drivers.

## 2. Upgrade Financed-Emissions Data

Move the largest Category 15 exposures — starting with **Utilities and Oil & Gas** — towards **PCAF data quality scores 1–2**.

Define an engagement or reallocation strategy for high-intensity investees.

## 3. Fix Calculation Defects Before External Reporting

Address the two identified calculation defects:

* **F1:** Rebuild and validate the spend-based emission factor library.
* **F2:** Correct the Category 5 emission factor unit basis.

## 4. Expand Supplier Engagement

Increase primary-data coverage in Category 1 beyond the current **22%**.

The objective should be to enable a more robust **hybrid calculation method** using supplier-specific and secondary data.

## 5. Capture Quick Wins

Pursue practical reductions in smaller categories where implementation is relatively straightforward:

* Shift **air freight to sea or rail** where lead times allow.
* Increase **landfill diversion** for end-of-life products.

# Executive Takeaway

The FY2023 Scope 3 inventory is dominated by **Use of Sold Products (Category 11)** and **Investments (Category 15)**, which together represent **99.8% of total Scope 3 emissions**.

The immediate priority is therefore twofold:

1. **Improve the accuracy and assurance-readiness of Categories 11 and 15**, where the overwhelming majority of emissions sit.
2. **Address the identified calculation and data-quality defects** before external reporting or assurance.

Upstream categories should continue to be maintained for completeness, but the largest opportunities for both **emissions reduction and inventory improvement** lie downstream — particularly in product use, product electrification, product efficiency, and financed emissions.
